### What it does
- `--record` starts `screencapture -v -x -C -k <file>` before the first check, so the pointer and clicks show in the video. It stops it after the last check.
- The file goes to `~/Library/Logs/hiwave/recordings/<YYYYmmdd-HHMMSS>-<label>-<checks>.mov`:
  - `--record-label` names the batch.
  - `<checks>` is `all` for the full set, the check names if there are up to three, and `<n>checks-<hash>` otherwise.
- A `--record-dir` inside the checkout is refused with exit 2, because the repo is public.
- **The recording stops in every exit path.** The normal end, a check that raises, and SIGTERM/SIGHUP all reach the run's outer `finally`; the two signals are turned into `SystemExit` for that. `--record-max-s` (default 2 h) stops a wedged run. Each stop sends the stop character first, then SIGINT, then kill, with a 30 s wait between steps.
- **`record` assertions in `result.json`.** The recording must cover the whole run, `screencapture` must exit with rc 0, and the .mov must exist and play with a video track. Its length must be within 5 s or 10% of the run. Playability is checked by the new `hwdrive movie <file>` (AVFoundation, no grant needed). `result.json` also gets `recording: {path, run_s, movie, stopped_by}`.
- **Without the Screen Recording grant, or with the screen locked:** `record` is NOT RUN and nothing is started.
- Not built: per-check clips (optional in the row). A `kill -9` of the driver can leave `screencapture` running; the README gives `pkill -INT -x screencapture` for that.
