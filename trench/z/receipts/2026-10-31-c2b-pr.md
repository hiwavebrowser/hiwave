## C2(b): a wheel moves the box under the pointer

PLAN-z row C2, slice (b). **Stacked on #845: land that first.** This PR's own commits are `54e2b947` (red) and `45292397` (fix); the rest of the diff is #845.

Until now the wheel only ever scrolled the window, so a scrolling box could be moved by script but not by the user.

### Rule, from Chromium 143.0.7499.4 driven by real wheel input
- The innermost scroller under the pointer that can move that way takes the whole delta. What it cannot use goes nowhere in that wheel.
- A box at its end hands on to the next scroller, then the window.
- `overflow: hidden` does not take the wheel. On a box with `overflow-x: auto; overflow-y: hidden`, a down wheel goes to the window and a diagonal one moves the box across and goes no further.
- An absolute box chains to its containing block's scroller, past a static scroller around it.
- The box hears one `scroll`, which does not bubble, and the document hears none.

### How
- The hit test now names the scrollers that move the hit box, innermost first (`HitTestResult::scrollers`), leaving out the ones an absolute or fixed box escapes.
- `Engine::scroll_at` picks the target. An element's scroll goes in whole pixels with the remainder carried, so trackpad steps smaller than a pixel add up. It paints first and tells the page after, as `scroll_view` does.
- The app's content-view wheel goes through a new `PageInput::ScrollAt`.

### Not from the oracle: the gesture hold
A run of wheels stays on what the first one moved, while the steps are under 300 ms apart. Without this, a flick on the page would be caught by any box passing under the pointer, and a flick in a box would run on into the page. Chromium holds real trackpad gestures on one target, but playwright's discrete wheels chain at once, so the oracle cannot show it: the two tests for it pin this engine's own rule, and 300 ms is my number, not a measured one. The input queue does not carry AppKit's gesture phases; using them would be better.

### Known gaps
- **No real-window run.** This seat has no input-posting grant. The engine tests call `scroll_at` directly; the path from `scrollWheel:` through `ScrollAt` is compiled, not run. The plan gives the real-window check to Atlas's seat.
- `overscroll-behavior` is not parsed: a `contain` box at its end hands on to the page, where Chromium stops.
- Keys and the window loop's wheel arm still scroll the window.
- Each wheel step on a box rebuilds the whole display list. Not timed on a large page; a long list may make box scrolling feel heavier than window scrolling.
- `scrollIntoView` and fragment navigation are slice (c), sticky in a scroller slice (d). The other gaps in #845 stand.
