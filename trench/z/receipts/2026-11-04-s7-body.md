## S7: a sibling combinator above the subject reads that ancestor's siblings

PLAN-z row **S7** (Handtest, eBay HAR): `input:checked + .icon` should hide one of each checkbox's two svg icons, but HiWave shows the checked icon on every box.

**Cause:** eBay's skin draws a checkbox as the `<input>` and then an icon span holding both svgs. It hides one of them with `.ctrl:checked + .icon .off` and `.ctrl:not(:checked) + .icon .on`. Here the `+` relates the icon span, which is an **ancestor** of the subject svg, to its earlier sibling. `selector_matches_prepared` always tested `+`/`~` against the **subject's** own `siblings_before`. Neither rule could match, so both icons showed, with the checked one on top. The same walk had two more bugs:
- Every chained sibling step was tested against the same sibling. `.t + .u + .v` missed and `.a ~ .bb ~ .c` matched in the wrong order.
- `:not(:checked)` on an earlier sibling was left permissive, so it matched a checked box.

The old gap was pinned in `script_selector_tests` ("Known gap … Chrome finds #deep").

**Fix (engine selector walk only):**
- The walk tracks where it stands. After a ` ` or `>` step lands on `ancestors[k]`, the next `+`/`~` reads **that element's** earlier siblings from the DOM (`dom_earlier_siblings`, found through `RELATIONAL_NODE` + `chain_ancestor_node`, checked by tag).
- Checkedness that has left the `checked` attribute (a click, a script) comes from `WalkCheckedScope`. It is set from the view's `checked_states` around every build (`LayoutCtx::with_building`), and from the query's map for `querySelector`/`matches` (only past the context-free early return).
- A sibling step that matched the sibling at j leaves only the siblings before j to the next step. `~` takes the nearest match, as the ancestor steps already do (no backtracking).
- `:not(:checked)`, `:not(:disabled)` and `:not(:enabled)` are decided on a sibling (`not_state`). On an ancestor they stay permissive, as before.
- A compound with a complex `:is()` member now names its node in `RELATIONAL_NODE` (`needs_node`), so the nested walk reads the right element's siblings.
